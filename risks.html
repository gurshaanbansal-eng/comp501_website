Harms & Ethical Concerns of Social Media Algorithms

Risks

This section covers the risks, harms, and ethical issues raised by social media algorithms. Content researched and written by Dilraj Singh Bhullar.

Misinformation & Filter Bubbles

Often, social media prioritises sensational or emotionally charged content over accurate information to increase engagement rate of users. Misinformation spreads quicker than corrections and false news spreads six times faster than true news. A huge factor contributing to spreading behaviour on social media is novelty and emotional arousal i.e. something that either surprises or excites the user (Vosoughi et al, 2018). The occurrence of such filter bubbles increases the chances of users getting repeatedly exposed to content that confirms their beliefs, thereby limiting them to views opposing their own (Vosoughi et al., 2018).

Mental Health Impacts

Heavy use of social media has been linked to negative mental health especially for young people. Following the advent of social media in 2010, the rates of suicide among teenagers in the US increased significantly. Studies show greater screen time on social media is a reason for this increase(Twenge et al., 2018). The extent of these platforms’ algorithmically driven logic intensifies young peoples’ exposure to social comparison and idealised contents, which cause further psychological harm (Twenge et al., 2018).

Privacy & Data Collection

The operation of algorithms requires massive amounts of data collection, such as recording clicks, dwell times, locations, and even social interactions in order to develop a profile for every individual. This data can be utilized for improving engagement maximization feeds, which in turn means that the more time spent by the individual using the platform, the better their targeting becomes. From the psychological perspective, the negative implications of constant surveillance and behavioral guidance have similar effects to those seen among adolescents (Twenge et al., 2018).

Algorithmic Bias

The training of algorithms based on past human-created data will result in the amplification of bias within society. Algorithms that are optimized for engagement are geared towards displaying information that generates an emotional response, which tends to include misleading and false information. Marginalized groups are particularly affected since they are likely targets of misinformation amplified through algorithms (Vosoughi et al., 2018).

Addiction & Excessive Use

From the start, platform designs have been engineered to maximise time on site by psychological triggers that manipulate our response to intermittent rewards. Social media platforms will generate a scrolling effect of addiction. However, increasing daily screen time has been associated with depressive symptoms, lonely feelings and thoughts of suicide for teens (Twenge et al., 2018). This impact shows how this design flaw is a physical hazard, even extending outside the scope of entertainment.
