What are Social Media Algorithms?

What Are Social Media Algorithms?

Every time you open Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube, an algorithm decides what you see first. These systems track exactly what you watch, skip, like, and share — then use that data to keep you scrolling as long as possible. Understanding how they work is the first step to understanding their impact on society.

How Do They Work?

Social media algorithms are sets of rules and machine learning models used by platforms to rank, filter, and personalise the content shown to each user. Rather than displaying posts in chronological order, they prioritise content based on predicted engagement — likes, comments, shares, and time spent viewing.

Key signals used by algorithms typically include: past interactions (what you've liked or commented on), content type preferences, time of posting, and the behaviour of similar users. Over time the algorithm builds a detailed profile of your interests and uses it to serve content it predicts will keep you on the platform longer.

Why It Matters

These algorithms are built to maximise engagement — not necessarily to support your wellbeing or show you accurate information. This has enormous consequences: from shaping political opinions and spreading misinformation, to impacting the mental health of teenagers and determining which small businesses succeed or fail.

As future computing professionals, understanding these systems means we can build more responsible technology and help society make better choices about how platforms are designed and regulated.

Platforms We Examined

  • TikTok — Uses a powerful recommendation engine driven by watch time and interaction patterns.
  • Instagram — Combines interest signals, relationship signals, and timeliness to rank the feed.
  • YouTube — Prioritises videos that maximise watch time and session duration.
  • Facebook — Factors in meaningful interactions, especially comments and shares from close connections.

Our Topic Focus

Our group — Group 32 — is investigating the social, ethical, and technical implications of social media algorithms. We explore the opportunities they create, the risks they pose, and the choices available to individuals, companies, and governments to manage their impact responsibly.
