Benefits & Potential of Social Media Algorithms

Opportunities

This section covers the opportunities and benefits that social media algorithms provide to individuals, businesses, and society. Content researched and written by Gurshaan Bansal.

Personalisation

Social media algorithms can offer great potential to provide individually tailored and targeted content according to individual preferences, interests and activities. Instead of a single feed with the same content for everybody, personalised content through algorithms provides appropriate material relevant for each user, making the platform experience better for the individual. Social media platforms are ideally suited to provide this at scale, where individually suited content is matched to passive audiences (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010). Offering relevant content can also strengthen an individual's relationship with communities and social networks consistent with their interests and social identities (Ellison et al., 2007).

Business & Content Discovery

Social media algorithms provide businesses with the unique ability to reach precisely the intended audiences to a much greater degree than through previous advertising models. This ability, and particularly the potential it brings to small to medium businesses to engage audiences against a smaller budget, can be considered a major defining opportunity of social media (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010). Consumers too can be said to benefit from this algorithmic discovery through the increased chance of being presented with goods, services or information that align with their past behaviour (Ellison et al., 2007).

Supporting Creators

Allows anybody to have content, and a following based purely on the drawing power, and quality of their own work, and not through the network or brand of a traditional publisher or network. Today, a niche content creator, whether it be for the purposes of educating, creating art or contributing to cultural discourse, can reach an audience worldwide through the algorithm. As Kaplan and Haenlein (2010) pointed out, "social media put the communicative power into the hands of the individual thereby creating completely new avenues for both self-expression and business" (p. 59). This notion is further supported by Ellison et al. (2007) who demonstrate that maintaining and developing relationships online through networked systems results in real world career and social capital.

Educational & Informational Access

The ability of algorithmic recommendation systems to provide access to education and information for users lacking formal educational resources is profound. Through interaction with learning-oriented material, an algorithmic system will propose related topics, feeding into self-perpetuating pathways of deeper subject knowledge. According to Kaplan and Haenlein (2010), social media removes the barrier of participation; anyone regardless of socioeconomic or geographic standing can be exposed to information and communities previously inaccessible. This is made worse by the network features of platforms where users connect, share data, and pass it on to others.(Ellison et al., 2007).

Community Building

One of the most meaningful opportunities presented by social media algorithms is their capacity to connect individuals who share common interests, identities or experiences regardless of geographic location. For people who are isolated, whether due to geography, disability, minority status or niche interests, algorithmically facilitated communities can provide genuine social support and belonging. As Ellison et al. (2007) pointed out, engagement with online social networks is positively associated with the formation of social capital, both in bridging connections across different social groups and deepening existing close relationships. This is further supported by Kaplan and Haenlein (2010) who emphasise that the core value of social media lies in its ability to unite users around shared content and interaction.
