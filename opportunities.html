Benefits & Potential of Social Media Algorithms

Opportunities

This section covers the opportunities and benefits that social media algorithms provide to individuals, businesses, and society. Content researched and written by Gurshaan Singh Bansal.

Personalisation

Social media algorithms can offer a great potential to provide individual tailored and targeted content according to individual's preference, interests and activities. Instead of a single feed with same content for everybody, personalized content through algorithms is a service that provides appropriate material relevant for each user, making the platform experience better for the individual. While other services also offer individual experience, social media platforms is ideally suited to provide this at a scale and a community context, where it offers individually suited content, broadcast to passive audiences (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010). Offering relevant content can also strengthen individual relationship with the communities and social networks that are consistent with individual's interests and social identities (Ellison et al., 2007).

Business & Content Discovery

Social media algorithms provide businesses with the unique ability to reach precisely the intended audiences to a much greater degree than through previous advertising models. This ability to reach defined audience groups, and particularly the potential this brings to small to medium businesses to engage audiences against a smaller budget compared to mass media, can be considered a major defining opportunity of social media according to Kaplan and Haenlein (2010). This new way of interacting with audiences offers business to promote presence, stimulate discovery, and communicate with consumers directly in a manner that small business did not have the financial resources to attain with the previously available methods of mass media advertising. While business benefits from this from targeting and direct interaction, consumers too can be said to benefit from this algorithmic discovery through the increased chance of being presented with goods, services or information that align with their past behaviour (Ellison et al., 2007).

Supporting Creators

Allows anybody to have content, and a following based purely on the drawing power, and quality of their own work, and not through the network or brand of a traditional publisher or network. Today, a niche content creator, whether it be for the purposes of educating, creating art or contributing to cultural discourse, can reach an audience worldwide through the algorithm. As Kaplan and Haenlein (2010) pointed out, "social media put the communicative power into the hands of the individual thereby creating completely new avenues for both self-expression and business" (p. 59). This notion is further supported by Ellison et al. (2007) who demonstrate that maintaining and developing relationships online through networked systems results in real world career and social capital.

Educational & Informational Access

The ability of algorithmic recommendation systems to provide access to education and information for users lacking formal educational resources is profound. Through a user interaction with learning-oriented material, an algorithmic system will propose related topics, feeding back on self-perpetuating pathways into deeper levels of subject knowledge. According to Kaplan and Haenlein (2010), the essence of the impact of social media on learning is the removal of the barrier of participation; anyone, regardless of socioeconomic or geographic standing, can be exposed to information and communities of users previously inaccessible. The impact of social media is compounded by the network characteristics of the platforms where information, rather than purely social communication, is exchanged and redistributed through the connections established by users (Ellison et al., 2007).

Community Building

One of the most meaningful opportunities presented by social media algorithms is their capacity to connect individuals who share common interests, identities or experiences regardless of geographic location. For people who are isolated, whether due to geography, disability, minority status or niche interests, algorithmically facilitated communities can provide genuine social support and a sense of belonging. As Ellison et al. (2007) pointed out, engagement with online social networks is positively associated with the formation of social capital, both in the bridging of connections across different social groups and the deepening of existing close relationships. This notion is further supported by Kaplan and Haenlein (2010) who emphasise that the core value of social media lies in its ability to unite users around shared content and interaction, suggesting that when the algorithm performs this function effectively, it is fulfilling the core promise of the technology itself.
